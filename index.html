From Jutland to Copenhagen:
Who Moves Where, and Why?

Copenhagen and Jutland are often framed as a cultural rivalry. We use 40+ years of district-level data to replace stereotypes with evidence: migration origin patterns, education profiles, and how they move together over time.

Research question: Do migrants from Jutland differ from locals in where they settle, and is Jutland presence higher in districts with higher education levels?

Story Structure

1. Map where Jutland-born residents are concentrated.
2. Compare district time trends and citywide shifts.
3. Test whether Jutland presence aligns with district education profiles.
4. Separate what the data supports from what it cannot prove.

Narrative genre: martini-glass style (guided explanatory flow + interactive exploration), following course discussions from Weeks 6-8.

Data Used

Geodata (`bydel.csv`)

District boundaries for Copenhagen (10 districts), used for choropleth mapping and spatial comparison.

Residents by Origin (`residents.xlsx` / KKBEF9)

Population by district, sex, age and birthplace (1977-2026). Used for Jutland-born and København-born shares.

Education (`education_attainment_dataset.csv` / KKUDD2)

Educational attainment by district and year (1985-2024). Used to compute district high-education share.

Merged district-year panel: 400 observations across shared years and districts.

Static Evidence Highlights

What We Can and Cannot Claim

We can show a robust district-level association between Jutland presence and district education profile.

We cannot directly infer individual-level education differences between Jutland-born and København-born people from this KKUDD2 extract, because it only includes the ancestry category Danish origin.
